# Results and Discussion

## 3.1 Partition-count agreement across observation systems

The proposed peak-selection and merging configuration improved the agreement between observed and WAVEWATCH III (WW3) partition counts for all three observing systems (Figure 2). The improvement is evident in the redistribution of cases toward the main diagonal of the contingency matrices, indicating a more consistent identification of the number of dominant wave systems. Diagonal agreement increased from 39.7% to 60.6% for SAR, from 40.3% to 47.5% for CFOSAT, and from 41.1% to 53.0% for NDBC. The corresponding Critical Success Index (CSI) increased from 0.398 to 0.606 for SAR, from 0.405 to 0.478 for CFOSAT, and from 0.414 to 0.537 for NDBC. Thus, the largest absolute and relative improvement occurred for SAR, whereas CFOSAT showed a more moderate but still systematic gain.

The contingency statistics support the same conclusion. The proposed configuration increased Cramér's $V$ from approximately 0.04 to 0.10 for SAR and from 0.11 to 0.18 for NDBC, while CFOSAT retained a similar association strength (approximately 0.19) despite the increase in diagonal agreement. Chi-square tests rejected independence between observed and modeled partition counts for every dataset and configuration ($p < 0.001$). Because the chi-square statistic depends on sample size as well as association, Cramér's $V$ and the diagonal/CSI metrics provide the more suitable basis for comparing effect magnitude among sensors. Together, these metrics show that the proposed configuration improves partition-count correspondence without a comparable change in conventional bulk-wave agreement (Figure 3).

The CFOSAT matrices in Figure 2 illustrate the progressive effect of the percentile criterion. The baseline configuration yields 40.3% diagonal agreement (1,579 of 3,914 collocations). Applying the P95 criterion produces only a small increase to 40.7% (1,591 cases), whereas P98 increases the diagonal fraction to 45.3% (1,774 cases) for the configuration displayed in the figure. The additional improvement reported for the final P98 configuration is therefore primarily attributable to peak selection; the merge factor acts as a secondary refinement. This distinction is important because the configurations shown in Figure 2 use a merge factor of 0.5, whereas the summary comparison in Figure 1 reports the final proposed setting.

## 3.2 Error structure and the role of percentile-based peak selection

The improvement is not a uniform reduction of every off-diagonal category. Rather, the proposed method preferentially reduces high-order disagreements, including cases in which two observed partitions correspond to three or more modeled partitions and cases involving three or more partitions on one side of the comparison. These categories represent the most severe form of partition-count disagreement because they imply fragmentation of the spectral structure into several candidate systems. Their reduction indicates that P98 suppresses weak local maxima before watershed segmentation and thereby limits the number of spurious basins entering the merging stage.

The remaining disagreements are concentrated more strongly in the $1\leftrightarrow2$ classes. Such cases are physically less severe than high-order fragmentation because they reflect ambiguity between one broad system and two partially resolved systems. In the CFOSAT example, the P98 configuration reduces the frequencies of the $2\rightarrow3+$ and $3+\rightarrow2$ categories relative to the baseline, while the $1\rightarrow2$ and $2\rightarrow1$ categories remain the main off-diagonal modes. This pattern is consistent with the intended function of the method: it does not force all spectra to have the same number of partitions, but reduces sensitivity to low-energy shoulders that are unlikely to constitute independent wave systems.

The percentile threshold and merge factor have distinct roles. The percentile criterion controls which local maxima are permitted to seed watershed basins, whereas the merge factor evaluates whether adjacent basins should be consolidated. Removing weak candidate peaks before segmentation reduces artificial fragmentation and reduces the number of pairwise decisions required during merging. If $n$ candidate peaks are retained, the number of possible pairwise relationships scales as $n(n-1)/2$; peak selection therefore improves both the physical interpretability and computational tractability of the partitioning problem. The results indicate that P98 is the main driver of the observed gain, with the merge factor refining the solution once weak secondary peaks have been excluded.

This result should not be interpreted as a modification of the modeled or observed spectral energy. Instead, it is a change in the representation used for partition comparison: the dominant maxima are retained, while weak maxima with limited relevance to the final partition structure are less likely to initiate independent basins. The resulting decrease in high-order disagreements therefore represents a more physically defensible comparison of multimodal spectra.

## 3.3 Spectral moments and bulk-parameter consistency

The spectral-moment analysis provides an independent explanation for the partition-count results (Figure 4). Across the three observation systems, agreement decreases as the moment order increases. The QQ plots show correlations of approximately 0.92--0.94 for $m_0$, 0.89--0.93 for $m_1$, and 0.84--0.93 for $m_2$. Departures from the one-to-one line become more evident in the upper quantiles, particularly for $m_2$. Since $m_0$ measures integrated spectral energy, whereas higher-order moments are increasingly sensitive to the distribution of energy across the spectral domain, this pattern indicates that WW3 represents total energy more consistently than detailed spectral shape.

This interpretation is consistent with the contingency analysis. Broader modeled spectral features and weak shoulders can generate additional local maxima and, consequently, extra watershed basins. The proposed percentile selection reduces the influence of these low-energy features during partition identification, while the close clustering of configurations in the Taylor diagrams indicates that the procedure does not materially alter the bulk statistical relationship between WW3 and the observations. Accordingly, the gain in CSI and diagonal agreement should be interpreted as an improvement in the robustness of spectral-system identification, not as an artificial improvement in wave-height, period, or wavelength skill.

## 3.4 Dependence on wave regime

The regime-stratified comparison of partition significant wave height ($H_s$) shows that WW3 performance depends on peak period (Figure 5). For NDBC, the intermediate regime ($12 \leq T_p < 16$ s; $n=8{,}159$) has the highest agreement, with $r=0.94$, RMSE = 0.67 m, and scatter index (SI) = 0.29. The long-period swell regime ($T_p \geq 16$ s; $n=1{,}901$) has similarly high correlation ($r=0.94$) but greater dispersion for energetic cases and SI = 0.35. This indicates that the occurrence of swell is represented well, while its amplitude and spectral organization become less certain as wave energy increases.

The short-period regime ($T_p < 12$ s; $n=41{,}394$) contains the largest number of collocations. It has RMSE = 0.49 m and a negative bias of -0.23 m, indicating an underestimation of locally generated wind-sea wave heights, although the correlation remains high ($r=0.90$). Equivalent qualitative behavior is observed for CFOSAT and SAR: the dominant partition is generally well reproduced, whereas secondary and tertiary systems account for a disproportionate share of the partition-count disagreement.

This contrast between good $H_s$ skill and imperfect partition-count agreement is a central result. Bulk parameters are dominated by the primary wave system and can therefore remain satisfactory even when the representation of secondary spectral maxima differs between model and observation. Partition-count metrics and higher-order moments reveal this additional information, demonstrating why bulk validation alone is insufficient for assessing the realism of multimodal directional spectra.

## 3.5 Spatial and seasonal consistency

The global distributions of partition properties confirm that the proposed procedure preserves the expected large-scale wave-climate structure (Figures 6--8). For the dominant partition, high $H_s$, long $T_p$, and long wavelengths occur over the Southern Ocean and the North Atlantic storm-track regions, while tropical and enclosed basins are characterized by lower-energy conditions. These patterns are reproduced across WW3, SAR, CFOSAT, and NDBC, indicating that peak selection does not introduce evident regional distortions in the dominant wave system.

The spatial coverage of detected partitions decreases from P1 to P3. P1 occurs across most oceanic regions, whereas P2 is concentrated where multiple systems coexist and P3 is restricted to comparatively complex sea states. The remaining differences are largest in energetic regions, particularly the Southern Ocean and major swell-propagation corridors, where multimodal spectra and interacting swell systems are common. The spatial error fields are coherent rather than random: for P1, mean $H_s$ RMSE is approximately 0.56 m for NDBC, 0.70 m for CFOSAT, and 0.92 m for SAR, and WW3 generally shows slightly negative bias in $T_p$ and wavelength. The similar broad-scale behavior across independent platforms suggests that these residuals principally reflect model and sea-state complexity rather than an artifact of the partition-selection procedure.

Seasonal maps provide a further consistency check. The analysis reproduces enhanced wave activity in the Northern Hemisphere storm tracks during January and the intensification of Southern Ocean wave energy during June. Because the corresponding patterns are retained in $H_s$, $T_p$, and wavelength, the method acts primarily on the identification of individual spectral peaks rather than on the geographical or seasonal structure of the wave climate.

Finally, WW3 identifies more partitions than the observations over broad areas of the Southern Ocean, North Atlantic, and North Pacific. This excess is strongly reduced when attention is restricted to energetic P1 events, for which single-partition conditions dominate. The result supports the interpretation derived from the contingency matrices and spectral moments: much of the initial disagreement is associated with weak secondary maxima that have limited contribution to integrated energy but a substantial influence on partition counts.

## 3.6 Implications

Across SAR, CFOSAT, and NDBC, the proposed configuration increases partition-count agreement while preserving the bulk and spatial properties of the dominant wave field. The strongest benefit is the reduction of high-order fragmentation errors, especially for SAR. The combined evidence from contingency metrics, spectral moments, regime-dependent scatter, and global distributions indicates that the main limitation in WW3--observation intercomparison concerns spectral organization rather than total wave energy. Adaptive percentile-based peak selection therefore provides a more robust basis for comparing physically meaningful wave systems across heterogeneous directional-spectrum products.
